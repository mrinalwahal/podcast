# Brand Specification

*Elaborates the brand section of the [Charter](charter.md). Normative: this defines who we are and how we sound. If it conflicts with the Charter, the Charter wins.*

## Identity (settled)

- **Show / channel:** runs under the host's name — **Mrinal Wahal** (YouTube channel = "Mrinal Wahal"). **No separate show name.** A short descriptive tagline may sit under it (e.g. *"Markets & freedom, explained honestly"*), but the brand is the person.
- **Language:** **Hinglish**, consistent — don't drift English↔Hindi between episodes.
- **What it is:** a Hinglish interview show whose spine is **monetary theory** — money, inflation, central banking, sound money — argued from an **anarcho-capitalist** conviction, opening onto the wider case for free markets and liberty. Treated as *ideas and theory*, never how-to.

## Positioning

**One-line:** *A show about monetary theory — what money is, why it loses value, and why the state shouldn't control it — made plain, in Hinglish, for a general Indian audience.*

**The hook:** an **anarcho-capitalist who takes the socialist seriously** — opens on a specific story, then works toward what money really is and who controls it. Radical in substance, inviting in method.

```
ACCESSIBLE ───────────────────────────────────────────────→ ACADEMIC
Mohak Mangal     Halan / Groww        [ US ]           Ideas of India   Seen & Unseen
explainers       personal finance     monetary theory   academic econ   3–6 hr, literary
mass, Hinglish   "how to invest"      ancap, Hinglish    ~90 min         (rival EiE ended)
                                      45–60 min, video
```

**The insight:** those shows differ in *subject*, not just level. Mohak = journalism; Halan/Groww = personal finance ("how to act with money"); the top of the column = *ideas*. **We're in the ideas column, with a spine no one else has: monetary theory from an anarcho-capitalist angle.** We borrow Halan's *warmth and trust*, not her *how-to* subject. Money is in scope **as theory** (what money is, why it's mis-managed, who benefits), never as stock tips.

**The whitespace we own** (four levers):
1. **Subject** — **monetary theory** (and the ancap/liberty case it opens onto) as *ideas*, not how-to, not news.
2. **Register & length** — warm Hinglish, tight 45–60 min.
3. **Format** — video-first, cumulative worldview-building.
4. **POV** — explicit **anarcho-capitalist**, money-first, **"pro-market, not pro-business" (anti-crony)**. Nobody owns "sound money vs. the state" for an Indian audience.

> **The opening:** *Everything is Everything* (the best smart pro-market show) ended Dec 2025 at Ep. 128 — its audience is orphaned. It had a credentialed economist permanently in the chair, so that audience expects rigor. Our host + expert-guest structure is built to deliver it.

## Audience

- **The aspirational graduate (primary)** — 22–32, first job / founder-curious; wants to *understand* the economy shaping their rent and salary.
- **The orphaned EiE listener** — ideas-literate; judges us on rigor + honesty; our loudest sharer.
- **The curious radical** — young Indian libertarians + the global **sound-money / Austrian / Bitcoin** audience; small but devoted, and they *share*.
- **The founder / operator** — feels regulation and cronyism directly; loves the anti-crony framing.
- **The curious professional / diaspora** — ideas-hungry, high-trust, and a natural sharer abroad; a distinctly Indian frame travels well to them.

**We say no to:** stock-tip seekers, personal-finance how-to seekers, hyper-partisans. Serving them dilutes the lane.

## Brand pillars (the promises kept every episode)

1. **Honest, not preachy** — concede market failures; celebrate progress *and* respect the pain.
2. **Pro-market ≠ pro-business** — against rigged markets and cronyism. Lead with it.
3. **The listener is the smartest person in the room** — no jargon survives untranslated.
4. **Indian, not imported** — jobs, prices, rent, small business, corruption, fairness — not Cato-branded libertarianism.
5. **Rigor you can feel** — real numbers, named sources, syllabus-grade show notes.

## Voice & tone spec

**We sound like:** a sharp, curious friend who did the reading — warm, plain-spoken, a little witty, unafraid to disagree kindly. Host talks 20–30%; the guest carries the ideas; the host carries the *clarity*.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Plain Hinglish, everyday examples | Seminar jargon, untranslated terms |
| "Choice," "a fair shot," "level playing field" | "Liberty/statism/collectivism" as slogans |
| Concede what's true even when inconvenient | Cheerlead; dodge the hard number |
| Curiosity, steelman, kind disagreement | Point-scoring, dunking, tribal signaling |
| Stories and specific numbers | Abstractions and vibes |

## Success metrics (honest expectations)

A niche ideas show is a **slow compounder**, not a viral rocket.

| Horizon | Reasonable target | What matters |
|---|---|---|
| Ep. 1–5 | Ship on schedule; find the voice; 2–3 clips outperform | Consistency + reps |
| Month 3–6 | Small loyal core; guest cross-promo working; newsletter growing | Loyalty & share-rate |
| Month 6–12 | Recognisable POV; guests start seeking *you* | Audience *quality* |
| Year 2 | Recognized authority — a body of work people cite | The show as an ideas engine |

**Ignore** month-1 subscriber counts. **Track** the unbroken publish streak, clip→episode funnel, newsletter growth, and guest-referred listeners.
