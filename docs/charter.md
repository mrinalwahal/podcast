# The Charter

*The source of truth for this venture. Every episode, guest, title, clip, and judgment call must conform to this document. When anything else in this repo conflicts with the Charter, the Charter wins.*

**Precedence:** `charter.md` → the specs (`brand.md`, `ideology.md`, `monetary-theory.md`) → the playbook (`format.md`, `distribution.md`, `craft.md`, `ecosystem.md`, `sources.md`) → individual episode material.
**Changing it:** the Charter is deliberately stable. Change it only on purpose, with a one-line note at the bottom — never as a side effect of another edit.

## What the show is

A **first-principles, analytical** interview show about **power, incentives, and freedom** — argued from an **anarcho-capitalist / classical-liberal** conviction, inviting in method. Hosted by **Mrinal Wahal**, Hinglish. **The through-line is the analytical lens, not any single topic** — running on two pillars, sequenced:

- **Pillar 1 — the politics of money (the launch beachhead):** who controls money, how that control is power, who it robs, why the state shouldn't have it. The sharpest niche nobody else owns — where we launch and earn an audience.
- **Pillar 2 — AI / deep learning (planned, host-led):** the host's own expertise, analysed through the *same* lens — compute as power, surveillance, incentives, competition. We broaden into it **only after the money beachhead lands.**

Felt topics (inflation, demonetisation, the digital rupee; and later, the AI you use every day) are **on-ramps to first principles** — never the destination.

> **It is about ideas and first principles, not the news.** Whatever the subject — money now, AI later — current data and day-to-day politics are *segues*, not subjects. If an episode ends where a news explainer would, we failed.

## Purpose (why this exists)

To bring a **rigorous, first-principles analytical lens** to **power, incentives, and freedom** in India — from a candid **anarcho-capitalist / classical-liberal** conviction — **honestly and accessibly**, to a country raised on statist and socialist assumptions. We start with the case for **sound money and free markets**, and extend the same lens to the technologies — AI — reshaping both. We exist to *persuade the curious skeptic and arm the curious radical*, not to flatter the already-convinced.

## North Star (one sentence)

> **Bring first principles to how power, incentives, and freedom actually work** — in plain Hinglish, for a general, skeptical, statist Indian audience — **starting with money** (what it is, why it loses value, why the state shouldn't control it) and extending to the technologies, like AI, reshaping both.

If a decision doesn't serve that sentence, it's probably wrong.

## Who we serve

- The **curious skeptic** — an aspirational, non-specialist Indian raised to trust the state, who feels something is wrong with money but was never given the theory.
- The **curious radical** — young Indian libertarians, and the global **sound-money / Austrian / Bitcoin** audience, who want rigor and a distinctly Indian frame.
- The ideas-literate audience orphaned by *Everything is Everything* (ended Dec 2025). Detail: [brand.md](brand.md).

## Scope — what's in and out

**In:** **Pillar 1 — the politics of money** (inflation as a hidden tax, the Cantillon effect, the state's money monopoly, CBDC/surveillance, sound money vs. fiat) and the **ancap / free-market / liberty** worldview it opens onto. **Pillar 2 (planned) — AI / deep learning**, through the same power/incentives/freedom lens (compute as the new scarce resource, algorithmic surveillance, Big-Tech incumbency, AI & wages). All treated as *ideas and first principles*, with felt topics as on-ramps.
**Out:** personal-finance how-to (stock/fund/crypto tips), blow-by-blow current politics, news-chasing, technical data for its own sake, the history of money for its own sake, partisan cheerleading — and, for AI, **product reviews, hype, and how-to tutorials** (we do the *political economy* of AI, not tool tips). Finance is in scope only as *theory* (why insurance is mis-sold; what money is), never as advice.
**Scale — launch narrow.** Pillar 1 opens with a **2-episode monetary arc** (the tax → the control & the way out), each a complete thought-chain; the **money beachhead runs first and earns the audience.** **Pillar 2 (AI) is planned, not launched** — we broaden into it only once the beachhead lands, host-led. One thought-chain per episode; history is *scaffolding*, not a subject.

## Fixed identity (settled — do not re-litigate without cause)

- **Channel:** "Mrinal Wahal" (personal brand). **No separate show name.**
- **Intellectual core:** a first-principles analytical lens on **power, incentives & freedom.** **Launch beachhead = monetary theory (Pillar 1); planned second pillar = AI / deep learning (Pillar 2 — the host's own expertise).** **Stance:** anarcho-capitalist / classical-liberal in conviction; inviting in method.
- **Language:** Hinglish, consistent.
- **Format:** one expert guest; **45–60 min**; **video-first**; **biweekly**, never missing the slot. **The host role flexes with the subject:** the **translator** on his curiosities (money — the guest carries the expertise), the **expert / peer** on his own turf (AI — the host carries it, or spars with a peer).
- **Opening:** every episode starts **cold, mid-conversation, on a specific story** — no *spoken* host/guest introductions, no "welcome to the show," no mission statement. But the **screen introduces**: an on-screen lower-third (name + credential) the first time each person appears is **required** — the ban is on spoken narration, not on visual context. On a new, no-reputation channel the guest's authority is *why* a skeptic gives you five minutes; show it even though you don't say it.
- **Closing:** every episode ends on the same **standardized ritual** — the capstone question → *"what's one question you hoped I'd ask, but didn't?"* → the guest's **book recommendation(s)** → *"who's the smartest person who disagrees with you?"* Keep recording past the goodbye. No corporate outro. (Detail: [format.md](format.md).)
- **Naming:** plain and descriptive — call things what they are. No slogans or clever episode titles.
- **Planning:** **guest-first** — pick the guest, then shape the topic to *their* expertise (within the remit). Recurring guests get numbered rounds, each a folder with a **host.md** (interviewer's sheet) and a **guest.md** (shareable brief).

## The Non-Negotiables (hard guardrails)

**Always:**
1. **Segue to first principles.** Start from felt pain (money now, AI later); end in the theory under the hood — first principles — not the news cycle.
2. **Follow the argument to first principles.** Steelman the statist/socialist view *first* (start where the listener is), then follow the logic honestly — landing the sound-money / ancap conclusion **when the argument earns it, not as a compulsory destination.** Radical in substance, inviting in method; when the honest answer is "this is genuinely hard," say that instead of forcing the bow-tie.
3. **Pro-market, not pro-business.** Separate *markets* from *incumbents* at least once per episode. **Incumbency is the enemy** — whether it's the state's money monopoly (Cantillon effect) or an entrenched Big-Tech platform.
4. **Steelman our own side too.** Concede the hard problems of sound money / gold / Bitcoin / statelessness (deflation, volatility, 19th-century bank panics, public goods). Honesty cuts both ways.
5. **Translate everything.** No jargon survives untranslated (the "smart 15-year-old" test).
6. **Anchor claims to a real, named source** and **verify live statistics** the week we record.
7. **One thought-chain per episode.** A complete argument, not a survey. Don't cram — if it needs more, it's 2–3 episodes, not one (cap prep at ~6–8 anchor questions).

**Never:**
1. Never spend an episode on inflation technicals, current data, or day-to-day politics for their own sake — they are on-ramps.
2. Never *open* with slogans or tribal labels ("abolish the RBI," "taxation is theft," "anarcho-capitalism") — earn them. Don't alienate before you persuade.
3. Never sound like an apologist for crony capitalism, big business, or a specific tycoon.
4. Never fabricate a figure, quote, or credential; never state an unverified live stat as fact.
5. Never drift into personal-finance/crypto advice or partisan tribalism.
6. Never publish content that impersonates a real person/organization or fabricates records.

## Decision rule (how to judge ANY choice)

Before greenlighting an episode, guest, title, clip, or framing, ask:

1. **Spine** — does it lead to **first principles** (the analytical lens), or dead-end in the news?
2. **Method** — does it start where the skeptic is and **follow the argument honestly** (landing the radical conclusion only when earned), rather than preach a predetermined verdict?
3. **Honesty** — does it concede what's true (including our own side's problems)? Would a thoughtful socialist feel respected?
4. **Accessibility** — can a non-specialist follow it without prior knowledge?
5. **Anti-crony** — does it defend *competition and the ordinary person*, not incumbents or the state's privileges?
6. **Evidence** — is every claim anchored and (if live) verified?

If any answer is "no," fix it or drop it.

## The Editorial Litmus Test (run on every episode)

- [ ] Did it **segue from a felt topic into first principles** (the theory under the hood)?
- [ ] Did we **steelman the statist view** before pushing our own?
- [ ] Did we **concede at least one hard problem** on our own side?
- [ ] Separated **markets from incumbents / the state's privileges** at least once?
- [ ] Every claim **anchored to a named source** (live stats verified)?
- [ ] Listener leaves with **one reframe about power, incentives, or freedom**, not a talking point?

Six "yes" = ready. Otherwise, not.

## Definition of done (an episode)

Recorded → passes the Litmus Test → **opens cold, mid-conversation, on a specific story (no intros)** → **closes on the standard ritual (capstone · "what should I have asked?" · the guest's book recs · who-to-book-next)** → chaptered → **syllabus-grade show notes (reading list, incl. the relevant canon + the guest's book recs)** written → 5–20 captioned clips queued → newsletter essay drafted → guest-share kit sent → next episode banked.
