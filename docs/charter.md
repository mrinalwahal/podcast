## What the show is

An **anarcho-capitalist** podcast whose intellectual spine is **monetary theory** — the nature of money, why it loses value, who controls it, and why the state shouldn't. Hosted by **Mrinal Wahal**, Hinglish, interview format. It uses felt, familiar topics (inflation, demonetization, the digital rupee, gold) as **on-ramps to theory** — never as the destination.

> **It is about monetary theory, not the news.** Inflation, current data, and day-to-day politics are *segues*, not subjects. If an episode ends where a news explainer would, we failed.

## Purpose (why this exists)

To make the case for **sound money, free markets, and individual liberty** in India — from a candid **anarcho-capitalist** conviction — **honestly and accessibly**, to a country raised on statist and socialist assumptions. We exist to *persuade the curious skeptic and arm the curious radical*, not to flatter the already-convinced.

## North Star (one sentence)

> **Explain monetary theory plainly** — what money is, why it loses value, and why the state shouldn't control it — to a general, skeptical, statist Indian audience.

If a decision doesn't serve that sentence, it's probably wrong.

## Who we serve

- The **curious skeptic** — an aspirational, non-specialist Indian raised to trust the state, who feels something is wrong with money but was never given the theory.
- The **curious radical** — young Indian libertarians, and the global **sound-money / Austrian / Bitcoin** audience, who want rigor and a distinctly Indian frame.
- The ideas-literate audience orphaned by *Everything is Everything* (ended Dec 2025). Detail: [brand.md](brand.md).

## Scope — what's in and out

**In:** **monetary theory as the spine** (what money is, inflation as a monetary phenomenon, the Cantillon effect, central banking, sound money vs. fiat, the state's money monopoly) and the broader **ancap / free-market / liberty** worldview it opens onto — treated as *ideas and theory*, with felt topics as on-ramps.
**Out:** personal-finance how-to (stock/fund/crypto tips), blow-by-blow current politics, news-chasing, technical data for its own sake, and partisan cheerleading. Finance is in scope only as *theory* (why insurance is mis-sold; what money is), never as advice.

## Fixed identity (settled — do not re-litigate without cause)

## The Non-Negotiables (hard guardrails)

**Always:**
1. **Segue to theory.** Start from felt pain; end in monetary theory / first principles. Not in the news cycle.
2. **Earn the radical conclusion.** Steelman the statist/socialist view *first* (start where the listener is), then push to the sound-money / ancap conclusion. Radical in substance, inviting in method.
3. **Pro-market, not pro-business.** Separate *markets* from *incumbents* at least once per episode. Cronyism is the enemy — and it is what the state's money monopoly *enables* (Cantillon effect).
4. **Steelman our own side too.** Concede the hard problems of sound money / gold / Bitcoin / statelessness (deflation, volatility, 19th-century bank panics, public goods). Honesty cuts both ways.
5. **Translate everything.** No jargon survives untranslated (the "smart 15-year-old" test).
6. **Anchor claims to a real, named source** and **verify live statistics** the week we record.

**Never:**
1. Never spend an episode on inflation technicals, current data, or day-to-day politics for their own sake — they are on-ramps.
2. Never *open* with slogans or tribal labels ("abolish the RBI," "taxation is theft," "anarcho-capitalism") — earn them. Don't alienate before you persuade.
3. Never sound like an apologist for crony capitalism, big business, or a specific tycoon.
4. Never fabricate a figure, quote, or credential; never state an unverified live stat as fact.
5. Never drift into personal-finance/crypto advice or partisan tribalism.
6. Never publish content that impersonates a real person/organization or fabricates records.

## Decision rule (how to judge ANY choice)

Before greenlighting an episode, guest, title, clip, sponsor, or framing, ask:

1. **Spine** — does it lead to monetary theory / first principles, or does it dead-end in the news?
2. **Method** — does it start where the skeptic is and *earn* the radical conclusion, rather than preach it?
3. **Honesty** — does it concede what's true (including our own side's problems)? Would a thoughtful socialist feel respected?
4. **Accessibility** — can a non-specialist follow it without prior knowledge?
5. **Anti-crony** — does it defend *competition and the ordinary person*, not incumbents or the state's privileges?
6. **Evidence** — is every claim anchored and (if live) verified?

If any answer is "no," fix it or drop it.

## The Editorial Litmus Test (run on every episode)

- [ ] Did it **segue from a felt topic into monetary theory / first principles**?
- [ ] Did we **steelman the statist view** before pushing our own?
- [ ] Did we **concede at least one hard problem** on our own side?
- [ ] Separated **markets from incumbents / the state's privileges** at least once?
- [ ] Every claim **anchored to a named source** (live stats verified)?
- [ ] Listener leaves with **one reframe about money and power**, not a talking point?

Six "yes" = ready. Otherwise, not.

## Definition of done (an episode)

Recorded → passes the Litmus Test → **opens cold, mid-conversation, on a specific story (no intros)** → chaptered → **syllabus-grade show notes (reading list, incl. the monetary canon)** written → 5–20 captioned clips queued → newsletter essay drafted → guest-share kit sent → next episode banked.
