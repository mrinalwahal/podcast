# Ideology & Editorial Specification

*Elaborates the ideology section of the [Charter](charter.md). Normative: this is how we argue, concede, and stay credible. If it conflicts with the Charter, the Charter wins.*

> In India, "pro-market" is routinely (and often correctly) heard as **"pro-Ambani/Adani."** If we don't actively separate *markets* from *incumbents*, we lose the room — and deserve to. This applies to the whole remit: money, economics, finance, policy, freedom.

## Our stance & method

**Our conviction is anarcho-capitalist**, and our sharpest, most defensible edge is monetary: **the state should not control money.** (Full case and canon: [monetary-theory.md](monetary-theory.md).)

**Our audience is a statist, socialist-raised India.** So the method is fixed — *radical in substance, inviting in method*:
- **Start where they are** — from a specific story or felt experience (demonetisation, the digital rupee, cash that buys less), not from doctrine.
- **Steelman the state first** — make the socialist case better than the socialist would, then walk it to its breaking point.
- **Earn the conclusion** — never *open* with "abolish the RBI," "taxation is theft," or "anarcho-capitalism." Reach them, with the listener nodding along the way.
- **Make money the spearpoint** — once someone sees the state quietly robbing and watching them *through money*, the wider questions (private law, private defense, is-tax-theft) open on their own. Route broader-liberty topics back through the money/incentives lens.

## The master frame: pro-market, NOT pro-business

- **Origin:** Rajan & Zingales, *Saving Capitalism from the Capitalists* (2003) — the deadliest enemy of free markets is the *incumbent capitalist* who, once big, rewrites the rules to kill competition.
- **The line** (K. Subramanian): India needs to be **"pro-business, not pro-crony."** Pro-market policy enables fair competition; pro-crony policy protects incumbents.
- **Why it works:** most Indians don't resent *markets* — they resent *rigged* markets. Attack cronyism and you're on the side of the aspiring. **Lead with it.**

**On air:** *"I'm not here to defend big business. I'm here to defend competition — which is exactly what big business, once it's big, tries to kill."*

## Five pitfalls that discredit us — and the fixes (guardrails)

1. **Apologist for cronyism / big business** → distance markets from incumbents; criticize specific monopolies, bailouts, and mis-selling by name.
2. **Tone-deaf about poverty/inequality** → respect the pain first, explain second.
3. **Ignoring market failures** → concede them out loud (health, education, pollution, insurance mis-selling).
4. **Preaching to the choir** → design every episode to persuade a *skeptic*, not high-five an ally.
5. **Leading with US-libertarian slogans/aesthetics** → we *hold* radical (ancap) conclusions, but don't *open* with "taxation is theft" or "government is the enemy," and drop the American cosplay (guns, Rand). Earn the radical conclusion from the listener's own experience, in Indian terms.

## What resonates in India

- **Bastiat's "seen and unseen"** as a *thinking tool*: every policy has a visible effect and invisible costs (the job "saved" by a tariff vs. jobs never created; rent "controlled" vs. housing never built). Reads as *analysis*, not cheerleading.
- **The 1991 story & the License Raj** — central planning → liberalization → mass poverty reduction (data: [Ep. 1 notes](../episodes/01-monetary-theory/notes.md)). Be honest it was *compounding* growth, not a one-year miracle.
- **Gurcharan Das's register** (*India Unbound*): the patriotic "unleash Indian energy" story — markets as pro-India and pro-poor.

## The honesty engine (our moat)

1. **Steelman the statist with their own yardstick (the bridge).** Use the **Kelkar–Shah test** (*In Service of the Republic*): before any intervention, ask *what market failure it fixes* and *whether the state even has the capacity to fix it.* Most fail on their own terms — a devastating argument *to a statist*, because it's made in their language. Then push further: if the state can't run the basics, why does it deserve a monopoly on money, law, and force?
2. **Steelman our own side too.** Concede the hard problems of sound money / gold / Bitcoin / statelessness (deflation, volatility, 19th-century bank panics, who-builds-the-roads). Honesty cuts both ways — otherwise we're just the other team's propaganda. (List: [monetary-theory.md](monetary-theory.md).)
3. **Defend the *system*, never the *outcome*.** Champion competition while attacking a specific monopoly or crony. When a connected firm wins, be loudest *against* it.
4. **Steelman method** (Dennett): restate the other side so well they say "yes, exactly" → note agreement → *then* challenge.

## Localize the vocabulary

| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Liberty / freedom" (as slogans) | "Choice," "a fair shot," "not being blocked" |
| "Statism / collectivism" | "The License Raj," "red tape," "permission to work" |
| "Free markets" (bare) | "Open competition," "a level playing field" |
| "Deregulation" | "Removing the rules that protect incumbents" |
| "Efficiency" | "More for the same money," "less waste" |
| "Government is the problem" | "A capable state doing fewer things well" |

Talk about **jobs, prices, rent, small business, corruption, fairness, money.**

## Fight a two-front war (stay un-brandable)

Criticize **both** sides from the *same* principle — the economic left (statism, over-regulation, price controls) **and** the nationalist right (majoritarianism, protectionism, "populist nationalism → crony capitalism"). Name *mechanisms*, not villains. More honest *and* strategically bulletproof.

## The editorial litmus test

The binding version lives in the [Charter](charter.md). In short, every episode must: **segue from a felt topic into monetary theory / first principles** · **steelman the statist before pushing our own view** · **concede one hard problem on our side** · **separate markets from incumbents / state privilege** · **anchor every claim** (verify live stats) · **leave the listener with one reframe about money and power.**

## The canon — voices, quotes & stories to draw on

*(The **monetary** canon — Menger, Mises, Hayek, Rothbard, free banking, Bitcoin — lives in [monetary-theory.md](monetary-theory.md). Below is the broader liberty canon. Verify exact wording against the original before putting a quote on screen — flags in [sources.md](sources.md).)*

**On cronyism (lead here):**
- **Raghuram Rajan** (2014): *"By killing transparency and competition, crony capitalism is harmful to free enterprise, opportunity, and economic growth."* Plus his "cycle of dependence" (poor → politician → businessman → poor).
- **Zingales**, *A Capitalism for the People*: the enemy is *"a pro-business (as opposed to pro-market) ideology."*
- **Arvind Subramanian** (Economic Survey 2017-18): India went *"from crony socialism to stigmatised capitalism"* — concedes Indian capitalism *earned* part of its bad name.

**On tone (tattoo this on your hand):**
- **Amit Varma:** harsh polemic *"alienates the neutral reader… respect the reader enough to let him come to his own conclusion without shouting it at him."*

**On not importing US libertarianism:**
- **Gurcharan Das:** don't *"succumb to the peculiar fantasy of the American Right that a nation's success derives simply from keeping the government out of the way… the state is of first-order importance."*

**On markets being *pro-poor*:**
- **CCS**, *Law, Liberty and Livelihood*: *"the absence of economic freedom is the main source of urban poverty."* Their street-vendor work fed the **Street Vendors Act, 2014.**

**On the flailing state:**
- **Rajagopalan & Tabarrok:** the Indian state *"flails because it is simultaneously too large and too small"* — over-regulating everything while unable to run any of it.

**Stories worth an episode each:** Nehru to JRD Tata — *"Never speak to me of profit. It is a dirty word"*; **Nani Palkhivala's post-Budget lectures filling a cricket stadium** (precedent for popularizing economics for the masses); the **1991 crisis** (~3 weeks of reserves) that forced reform.

**The honesty flag to raise before a critic does:** post-1991 gains were **uneven** (~46% of the poor are in UP/Bihar/Maharashtra), and **real wages for many workers have been roughly flat 2017–2024** even as GDP grew. Say it yourself — it makes everything else more believable.
