# Interview Craft & Dos/Don'ts

*Synthesized from Transom, Ira Glass, Tim Ferriss/Cal Fussman, Lex Fridman, Amit Varma, and journalism guides.*

> **Your job in the chair:** not to be the smartest person in the room — to be the **relentless translator** for a listener who isn't your friend and didn't study economics. Everything here serves that.

## The arc (any length)

Open cold, mid-conversation, on a **specific story** (no host/guest intros, no "welcome to the show") → **three gears** (the story / felt world → mechanism → stakes) → 2–4 turn points → the standard close ritual + one takeaway. *(Ira Glass: a story = **plot**, one thing leading to the next, + **ideas**, what it means. Keep both moving — and you're literally starting on a story.)*

**Long (60–90m) vs tight (40–50m):** for a first episode and unknown chemistry, **lean tight.** Go long only when it's clearly flying.

## Question design (where the craft lives)

- **Open beats closed.** "How / what / why / tell me about…" opens a story; "did you / is it true…" dead-ends. Use closed only to nail a fact.
- **Get scenes, not summaries.** "Tell me about the moment you realized…" The most reliable radio question (Ira Glass): *"How did you think it would work out — and how did it actually work out?"*
- **Heart → head → soul** (Fussman): emotion and stakes *before* abstraction.
- **The "explain it to a 15-year-old" move (your signature).** Say it out loud: *"No equations, no jargon — the everyday-life version."* When jargon slips, **stop and translate on the spot.**
- **Follow-ups are the interview.** Echo to pull more ("Wait — say more"); **ask the same thing 4–5 different ways**; **force specificity** ("Is it more like *this* or *that*?").
- **Steelman before you challenge** (Dennett): restate the other side so well they say "yes, exactly," note agreement, *then* push. Best tape + most credible.
- **Kill two failure modes:** **leading questions** ("Don't you think inflation punishes the poor?" → "Who does inflation hit hardest, and why?") and **double-barreled questions** (ask one thing, get the answer, *then* the next).
- **One thought-chain per episode.** 6–8 anchor questions, not a survey. If it needs more, it's 2–3 episodes — don't cram.

## Hosting technique

- **Listen to the answer, not your next question** — the #1 rookie failure. Your prep earns the right to *abandon* it.
- **Let silence do the work** — wait a beat; people fill it with the honest thing.
- **Talk ~20–30%, guest 70–80%.** Short questions; cut the clever ones to the verb. Nod instead of "mm-hmm" so the tape runs clean.
- **Riff, don't read** — bullets, not a script. (Fridman: ask what you genuinely want to know; let the audience watch you learn.)
- **Interrupt only for the listener** — to translate jargon, rescue a tangent, or mark a great line. Never to be clever.
- **Build rapport off-mic** — "what would make this a home run for you?"; tell them it's fine to fumble and restate.

## First-episode specifics

- **Launch with 2–3 episodes** (fans want to binge).
- **Record Ep. 1 last** — you'll be visibly better after reps.
- **No intro, no mission statement** — open straight on a specific story, mid-conversation. Let the show explain itself by doing.
- **Treat Ep. 1 as a pilot** — prove the format: can you make an expert clear and human in ~45 min?
- **Give one takeaway** — a reframe they didn't have before.

## The master DOs and DON'Ts

**DO** — open cold, mid-conversation, on a specific story · start concrete, then climb to theory · ask **one** thing at a time · "explain it to a 15-year-old" · wait through silence · follow the surprising thread · steelman before challenging · separate **markets from incumbents** once · concede one thing your side gets wrong · anchor claims to a number/source · prep deeply, then *listen* · one memorable takeaway · keep recording past the "end."

**DON'T** — introduce yourself or the guest · say "welcome to the show" or state a mission up front · read like a checklist · leading/double-barreled questions · fill every pause · out-talk the guest · let jargon slide · nod along to a claim you'd challenge · **slip into cosy in-jokes with a friend-guest** (the listener is shut out) · sound like an apologist for big business · over-edit Ep. 1 into never shipping.

## The prep-doc template (a map you can abandon)

```
EPISODE __ : [TOPIC] with [GUEST]
Promise to the listener: ________________

A. RESEARCH NOTES (for me, not read aloud)
   - Guest's work read: __  · The glossed-over detail to open on: __
   - "What would make this a home run for you?" (ask in advance): __
B. THE SPINE — 6–8 anchor questions in arc order (accessible → mechanism → stakes)
   1. [Gear 1] __   2. ...   (one-line openers, doorways not paragraphs)
C. BACKUP QUESTIONS — to reignite energy / fill time
D. TRANSITIONS & RESCUE LINES (pre-written)
   - "We've talked about what X feels like — let's go under the hood."
   - "For anyone who just joined that thought…"
E. JARGON & LANDMINE LIST — terms I'll force the guest to translate
F. CLOSE (standard ritual) — capstone question · "what should I have asked?" · book rec(s) · who-disagrees · keep rolling; + the one-line takeaway
G. LINES TO LAND — punchy, honest one-liners + quirky words/Hinglish to use for attention & clips (on-air delivery, not slogans-as-openers)
```
