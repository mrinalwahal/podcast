# Monetary Theory — Subject & Canon Spec

*The intellectual spine of the show. Elaborates the Charter's scope. We do the **politics of money** — who controls it, how that control is power, who it robs, and what it means for freedom — **not** the history or mechanics for their own sake. History (barter, cowries, 1971, Yap) is **scaffolding**: use it only where it serves the political point, briefly.*

## The core thesis (in one paragraph)

Money is not a creation of the state — it emerged from markets. But when the state monopolizes money (central banking, fiat, legal-tender laws), it gains the power to quietly tax through inflation, to redistribute to whoever gets the new money first (the **Cantillon effect** — usually the state and the connected), and — with digital money — to surveil and switch off individual spending. **Sound money limits state power; state money enables it.** That is the show's spearpoint, and the cleanest, most defensible edge of the broader anarcho-capitalist case.

## The arc: monetary theory in 2 episodes (each a political thought-chain)

| Ep | Political claim | Core ideas |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Inflation as a hidden tax** *(the robbery)* | Money is debased on purpose; it's an unlegislated, regressive tax that funds the state and the connected off your savings | Inflation is monetary · Cantillon effect · seigniorage / debt erosion / financial repression |
| **2 — Money without the state** *(the control, and the way out)* | The monopoly is *control* — cancel, program, or surveil your money — so the answer is money the state can't touch | Money monopoly · demonetisation · CBDC · sound vs. fiat · separation of money & state · gold, competing currencies, Bitcoin (with honest problems) |

## Key ideas (the recurring political "frames")

| Idea | Plain version | Indian on-ramp |
|---|---|---|
| **Inflation is a hidden tax** | Printing money quietly transfers value from savers to the printer — no vote required | Financial repression; the saver vs. the state |
| **Cantillon effect** | Whoever gets new money *first* wins; the poor get it last, at higher prices | Crony credit; who benefits from cheap loans |
| **The money monopoly** | Why should one institution alone create the money you're forced to use? (Hayek) | The RBI; legal tender; demonetisation |
| **Money as control / CBDC** | State money that can watch, restrict, or switch off every transaction | The Digital Rupee (e₹) |
| **Sound vs. fiat money** | Money the state can't debase or seize vs. money it can | Gold in Indian homes; the rupee's slide; Bitcoin |
| **Separation of money & state** | Take money out of state hands: gold, competing currencies, Bitcoin | India's crypto fights; dollarisation abroad |
| *Scaffolding — use sparingly:* **origin of money** (Menger), **fiat turn** (1971), **Austrian business cycle**, **free banking history** | how money arose / how it broke / booms & busts | cowries & gold; NPAs & 2008; Scotland 1716–1845 |

## The canon (draw on it for the *political* argument; steelman the other side)

**Our side (sound money / Austrian):**
- **Murray Rothbard** — *What Has Government Done to Our Money?* (the political-monetary core, accessible).
- **F. A. Hayek** — *The Denationalisation of Money* (take money away from the state).
- **Jörg Guido Hülsmann** — *The Ethics of Money Production* (the explicitly *moral/political* case against fiat).
- **Ludwig von Mises** — *The Theory of Money and Credit*; inflation as debasement.
- **Saifedean Ammous** — *The Bitcoin Standard* (separation of money and state, modern framing).
- **Richard Cantillon** — the original of the "first-receiver" effect. · **Carl Menger** — origin of money (scaffolding).

**The other side (steelman, don't strawman):** Keynes and post-Keynesians; **MMT**; the mainstream central-banking / inflation-targeting case (lender of last resort, elastic money); **Friedman** as the monetarist bridge (inflation is monetary — but keep the central bank). *(Keynes is quotable for us too: governments can "confiscate, secretly and unobserved, an important part of the wealth of their citizens" via inflation.)*

**Indian voices & material:** Vivek Kaul's *Easy Money* trilogy (accessible monetary history); the RBI framework (for the steelman); the demonetisation literature; India's gold economics; the Digital Rupee (e₹) pilots.

## Honest counterweights (concede these on air)
- **Deflation risk** of hard money; the debate over whether gentle inflation "greases" a growing economy.
- **Volatility** of gold/Bitcoin as everyday money; the **adoption / regression** problem.
- **19th-century bank panics** — free banking's real historical blemishes (and the free-bankers' replies).
- **Public goods / the stateless money order** — who stops fraud and enforces contracts without a state? Take it seriously.
- India-specific: a poor, informal economy's reliance on cash *and* on state welfare rails — the radical case must reckon with this, not wave it away.

## How money connects to the whole ancap case
Money is the *thin end of the wedge*: once a listener sees that the state's control of money quietly robs and surveils them, the broader questions open naturally — Is taxation theft? Can law and defense be private? Why does a bigger state breed more cronyism? **Start at money; travel outward** — and route broader-liberty topics back through the money/power lens so the show stays coherent, not a generic libertarian channel.
