# Monetary Theory — Subject & Canon Spec

*The intellectual spine of the show. Elaborates the Charter's scope. Use this to build episodes: every topic should trace back to one of these ideas, land through an Indian on-ramp, and stay honest about the counterarguments.*

## The core thesis (in one paragraph)

Money is not a creation of the state — it emerged from markets as the most saleable good. When the state monopolizes money (central banking, fiat, legal-tender laws), it gains the power to quietly tax through inflation, to redistribute to whoever gets the new money first (the **Cantillon effect** — usually the state and the connected), to distort interest rates and cause boom-and-bust cycles, and — with digital money — to surveil and control. **Sound money limits state power; state money enables it.** That is the show's spearpoint, and the cleanest, most defensible edge of the broader anarcho-capitalist case.

## Key ideas (the recurring "frames")

| Idea | Plain version | Indian on-ramp |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin of money** (Menger) | Money emerged from barter as the most tradeable good — no king required | Cowries, gold, the rupee's history |
| **Inflation is monetary** (Friedman/Mises) | Prices rise because money is printed, not because sellers got greedy | The rupee buying less; RBI balance sheet |
| **Inflation as a hidden tax** | Printing money transfers value from savers to the printer | Financial repression; the saver vs. the state |
| **Cantillon effect** | Whoever gets new money *first* wins; the poor get it last, at higher prices | Crony credit; who benefits from cheap loans |
| **Sound vs. fiat money** | Money the state can't debase vs. money it can | Gold in Indian homes; 1971; the rupee's slide |
| **Austrian Business Cycle Theory** | Artificially cheap credit fuels booms that must bust | India's NPA/credit cycles; 2008 |
| **The money monopoly** | Why should one institution alone create money? (Hayek) | The RBI; legal tender; demonetization |
| **Free banking** | Competing private currencies / banks, no central bank | Scotland 1716–1845; a thought experiment for India |
| **Hard money & Bitcoin** | Digitally scarce money outside state control | India's crypto tax/ban fights; UPI vs. Bitcoin |
| **CBDC / programmable money** | State money that can watch and restrict every transaction | The Digital Rupee (e₹) |

## The canon (thinkers & texts to draw on and to steelman against)

**The sound-money / Austrian tradition (our side):**
- **Carl Menger** — *On the Origins of Money* (money as spontaneous order).
- **Ludwig von Mises** — *The Theory of Money and Credit*; the regression theorem; inflation as debasement.
- **F. A. Hayek** — *The Denationalisation of Money* (competing private currencies); *Prices and Production*.
- **Murray Rothbard** — *What Has Government Done to Our Money?*; *The Mystery of Banking* (the ancap monetary core, accessible).
- **George Selgin / Lawrence White / Kevin Dowd** — *free banking* (money without a central bank; the empirical cases).
- **Saifedean Ammous** — *The Bitcoin Standard* (hard money, modern framing).
- **Richard Cantillon** — the original of the "first-receiver" effect.

**The other side (steelman these, don't strawman them):**
- **Keynes** and post-Keynesians; **Modern Monetary Theory (MMT)**; the mainstream central-banking / inflation-targeting case (why a lender of last resort and elastic money exist); **Milton Friedman** as the monetarist bridge (inflation is monetary — but keep the Fed).

**Indian voices & material:** RBI monetary-policy framework and history; Vivek Kaul's *Easy Money* trilogy (accessible monetary history); the demonetization literature; India's gold economics; the Digital Rupee (e₹) pilots; the 1991 crisis and the gold airlift.

## Honest counterweights (concede these on air)

- **Deflation risk** of hard money; the debate over whether gentle inflation "greases" a growing economy.
- **Volatility** of gold/Bitcoin as everyday money; the **regression/adoption** problem.
- **19th-century bank panics** — free banking's real historical blemishes (and the free-bankers' replies).
- **Public goods / the stateless money order** — who enforces contracts and stops fraud without a state? Take it seriously.
- India-specific: a poor, informal economy's reliance on cash *and* on state welfare rails — the radical case must reckon with this, not wave it away.

## How money connects to the whole ancap case

Money is the *thin end of the wedge*: once a listener sees that the state's control of money quietly robs and surveils them, the broader questions open naturally — Is taxation theft? Can law and defense be private? Why does a bigger state breed more cronyism? **Start at money; travel outward.** Broader-liberty episodes should still route *through* the monetary/incentives lens so the show stays coherent, not a generic libertarian channel.
