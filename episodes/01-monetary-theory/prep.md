## OPEN ON A STORY (pick one; mid-conversation, no intros)
- **The POW camp (Radford, 1945).** In a WWII prison camp — no government, no bank — cigarettes became money: prices, credit, even "counterfeiting" by wetting tobacco to bulk it up. Money *emerges* from markets, not the state. → *"so if money can appear with no government at all… what actually IS money?"*
- **The stone money of Yap.** Giant limestone discs (Rai) as money — too heavy to move, so ownership changed while the stone stayed put; one famous stone sank at sea and still counted as wealth. Money = a shared ledger of who owns what. → *"so is the rupee any different?"*
- **Demonetisation night, 8 Nov 2016.** 8pm: the PM goes on TV; by midnight ₹500 & ₹1,000 notes stop being money — ~86% of the country's cash by value, void overnight. → *"wait — who has the power to decide what counts as money?"*

## THE ARC (4 gears)
1. **The story** — one of the above (brief, concrete)
2. **What is money?** — why do we accept this paper at all? (Menger: money is *discovered*, not decreed)
3. **Why does it lose value?** — the quantity of money; the **Cantillon effect** (who gets new money first)
4. **The radical turn** — should *any* institution have the power to print the money you're forced to use? (steelman the RBI → demonetisation → digital rupee → sound-money alternatives, honestly)

## 5 THINGS TO LAND
1. Money **emerged from markets** as the most saleable good — the state captured it, it didn't create it.
2. Inflation is a **monetary** phenomenon (too much money), not sellers being greedy.
3. **Cantillon effect:** whoever gets new money *first* wins; the ordinary person gets it last, after prices rise. Inflation = a hidden, regressive tax nobody voted for.
4. Whoever controls money controls far more than prices — **stability, surveillance, and power.**
5. **Sound money** (gold, competing currencies, Bitcoin) limits that power — and has real trade-offs we'll name honestly.

## THE SPINE (story → theory → radical)

**Gear 1 — the story / concrete (keep short)**
1. [From the opening story] …so how did a thing nobody was ordered to use become money?
2. Closer to home: ₹4 to a dollar in 1947, ₹80+ today. What happened in between?

**Gear 2 — what IS money?**
3. Forget inflation — what *is* this paper in my wallet, and why does the chaiwallah accept it? *(Menger: emergent, not decreed)*
4. Money used to be gold, silver, cowries — things nobody could just print. When and why did it become paper the state prints at will? *(fiat; 1971)*
5. So who actually *creates* rupees today, and how? *(RBI + banks; "printing" isn't literal)*

**Gear 3 — why it loses value (the theory)**
6. Explain to a 15-year-old: why do prices rise across the board, year after year — greed, or something upstream? *(quantity of money)*
7. The bit nobody hears: when new money is created, who gets it **first** — and why does that matter? *(Cantillon: first-receivers win)*
8. So is inflation basically a tax nobody voted for? Who's the taxman, and who pays most? *(hidden, regressive — ties to anti-crony)*

**Gear 4 — the radical turn (earn it; steelman first)**
9. Steelman it for me: why do we *have* a central bank at all — the best case for the RBI controlling money? *(stability, lender of last resort, deflation fear)*
10. Now the uncomfortable part: what did that power let the state do overnight on 8 Nov 2016? *(demonetisation)*
11. Where does it go next — a programmable **digital rupee** the state can watch, and switch off? What would that do to a free society?
12. If money didn't have to come from the state — gold, competing currencies, Bitcoin — could it work? Be honest: what breaks? *(concede deflation, volatility, panics)*

**Close**
13. If every Indian understood one thing about money, what would it be? *(recurring closing question → best clip)*
