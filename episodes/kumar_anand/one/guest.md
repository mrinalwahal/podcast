# For Kumar — our first recording: *the monetary arc* (one sitting, two episodes)
*Share with the guest. Host's own sheet: [host.md](host.md).*

**Gist:** our experimental first recording — relaxed, low-stakes, learning as we go. We do it as **one long conversation** and publish it as **two episodes.** The subject is money as a *political* thing — not CPI prints, not tips.

**Two acts, one chat:**
- **Act 1 — Inflation as a hidden tax:** money loses value on purpose → whoever prints it spends it first, you get it last → **a tax nobody voted for.** *Your public-finance angle (how the state funds itself) is the heart of it.*
- **Act 2 — Money without the state:** the monopoly is *control* (demonetisation, a programmable digital rupee) → so *why should the state control money at all?* → gold / competing currencies / Bitcoin — and can it work? *Your economic-historian's stories (the rupee, demonetisation, the RBI) are what I want.*

**How I run it:**
- No intros — we start mid-conversation, on a story, and just keep going from Act 1 into Act 2.
- I'll stop you for the plain-Hinglish version of any jargon (a feature, not doubt).
- I'll steelman the RBI / the state / the digital rupee — help me make it strong, then we push back.
- We concede our own side too (deflation, rigidity; gold was seized in 1933; Bitcoin's volatile; the poor rely on cash + welfare). Honesty is the brand.
- **No investment/crypto tips** — money-as-freedom, not trades.

**Come with (if you get time):**
- An opening **debasement story** (Weimar / Keynes-on-Lenin / Roman coins / your own) **and** a **control story** (demonetisation night is gold — your memories of it).
- Concrete Indian examples of **who gets new money first.**
- Your **real view**: gold vs. competing currencies vs. Bitcoin, *for India* — what breaks first.
- One **number** that surprised you; one thing you **changed your mind** about; a **book or two** to recommend.

**Not this time:** the CPI basket / latest print; stock/crypto tips.

**I close by asking:** a book rec, *"what's one question you hoped I'd ask, but didn't?"*, and *"one reform you genuinely wish someone was pushing — but no one is?"*

**Logistics:** one sitting, **~90–120 min** (we'll wander — no pressure), Hinglish, video (in-person or Riverside).

**Optional skim:** Rothbard, *What Has Government Done to Our Money?* · Vivek Kaul, *Easy Money* · Hayek, *The Denationalisation of Money* · Ammous, *The Bitcoin Standard*.
