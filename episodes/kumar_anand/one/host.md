## The thought-chain (what I'm steering toward)
Money is debased **on purpose** → whoever prints it spends it first (Cantillon), you get it last → inflation is a wealth transfer from savers to the state and the connected → **a tax nobody voted for.**

## Open on a story (no intros — mid-conversation)
- **Keynes on Lenin**, *Economic Consequences of the Peace* (1919): inflation as a deliberate weapon to "debauch the currency." ⚠️ verify wording.
- **Weimar 1923** — paid twice a day; the wheelbarrow worth less than the notes.
- **Roman coin-clipping** — emperors cutting the silver in the denarius to fund wars.

## The spine (6–8 anchor questions; story → politics → judgement)
1. [from the story] …so is inflation something that just happens, or something someone *does*?
2. Your rupee buys less every year — law of nature, or policy choice? Who makes it? *(printed; the ~4% target)*
3. When new money is created, who spends it **first**, and who gets it **last** — why does the order rob you? *(Cantillon)*
4. In India, name the first-receivers vs. the last. *(govt, banks, borrowers, asset-owners → vs. wage-earners, pensioners, cash savers)*
5. So is inflation a tax? Who levied it, who voted for it, who pays most? *(unlegislated, regressive)*
6. **His wheelhouse:** why does a government reach for inflation instead of honest taxes? *(seigniorage; deficit financing; inflating away its own debt — invisible, no vote, someone else gets blamed)*
7. Steelman it: the honest case that a little inflation is necessary — even good. Does it hold? *(deflation; elastic money)*
8. **Close:** if every Indian saw inflation as a tax, what would change?

## Steelman first, then judge
Make the statist case strong (deflation is destructive; lender of last resort; elastic money; a buffer from the zero bound) — *then* push: does that justify a **permanent, unlegislated, regressive transfer** at discretion? Could a **rule** or **hard money** get the benefit without the discretion?

## Concede our own side
Zero-inflation/hard money has real risks (deflation, rigidity); and *some* of any price rise is real supply shocks/growth, not pure debasement. Don't overclaim "all inflation is theft." Accuracy is the brand.

## Takeaway to steer toward
*"Inflation isn't weather. It's a tax you never voted for — collected by whoever runs the printing press, and paid most by those furthest from it."*

## Key facts ⚠️ verify the week you record
- RBI **targets 4% inflation** (2–6% band) — the erosion is *deliberate policy.*
- ~4%/yr roughly **halves the rupee's purchasing power every ~17–18 years** (rule of 70 — present as arithmetic).
- Governments earn **seigniorage** and **inflate away the real value of their own debt** — two political reasons to do it.
- Rupee **~₹4/$ (1947) → ₹80+/$ (2025)** — the visible trail of debasement.
