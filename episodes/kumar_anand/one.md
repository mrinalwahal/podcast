## The thought-chain
Money is debased **on purpose** → whoever prints it spends it first (Cantillon), you get it last → so inflation is a wealth transfer from savers to the state and the connected → **a tax nobody voted for.** *(Kumar's public-finance angle: it's the tax a government reaches for when it can't or won't tax openly.)*

## Open on a story (no intros — mid-conversation)
- **Keynes on Lenin**, *The Economic Consequences of the Peace* (1919): inflation as a deliberate weapon to "debauch the currency" — a *statist* admitting it. ⚠️ verify wording.
- **Weimar 1923** — paid twice a day; the wheelbarrow worth less than the notes in it.
- **Roman coin-clipping** — emperors cutting the silver in the denarius to fund wars; the original hidden tax.

## The spine (6–8 anchor questions; story → politics → judgement)
1. [from the story] …so is inflation something that just happens, or something someone *does*?
2. Your rupee buys less every year — law of nature, or policy choice? Who makes it? *(printed; the ~4% target)*
3. When new money is created, who spends it **first**, and who gets it **last** — and why does the order rob you? *(Cantillon)*
4. In India, name the first-receivers vs. the last. *(govt, banks, borrowers, asset-owners → vs. wage-earners, pensioners, cash savers)*
5. So is inflation a tax? Who levied it, who voted for it, who pays most? *(unlegislated, regressive)*
6. **Kumar's wheelhouse:** why does a government reach for inflation instead of honest taxes? *(seigniorage; deficit financing; inflating away its own debt — invisible, no vote, someone else gets blamed)*
7. Steelman it: the honest case that a little inflation is necessary — even good. …Does it hold? *(deflation risk; elastic money)*
8. **Close:** if every Indian saw inflation as a tax, what would change?

## Steelman first, then judge (the honesty engine)
Make the statist case strong: **deflation** can be destructive (debt spirals, deferred spending); a **lender of last resort** and **elastic money** manage shocks; a small positive target keeps a buffer from the zero bound. *Then* push: does that justify a **permanent, unlegislated, regressive transfer** at discretion? Could a **rule** or **hard money** get the benefit without the discretion?

## Concede our own side
Zero-inflation/hard money has real risks (deflation, rigidity); and be precise — *some* of any price rise is real supply shocks or growth, not pure debasement. Don't overclaim "all inflation is theft." Accuracy is the brand.

## Takeaway to steer toward
*"Inflation isn't weather. It's a tax you never voted for — collected by whoever runs the printing press, and paid most by those furthest from it."*

## Key facts ⚠️ verify the week you record
- RBI **targets 4% inflation** (2–6% band) — the erosion is *deliberate policy.*
- ~4%/yr roughly **halves the rupee's purchasing power every ~17–18 years** (rule of 70 — present as arithmetic).
- Governments earn **seigniorage** from creating money and **inflate away the real value of their own debt** — two political reasons to do it.
- Rupee **~₹4/$ (1947) → ₹80+/$ (2025)** — the visible trail of debasement.
